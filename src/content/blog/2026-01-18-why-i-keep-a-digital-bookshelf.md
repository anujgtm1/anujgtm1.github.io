For years I tracked reading the way people track step counts: a numeric target for December 31st and a tiny jolt of relief whenever a shorter novella nudged the counter forward. The unintended side effect was subtle but real — I started avoiding dense, rewarding books simply because they moved the progress bar too slowly.

> A book is not a receipt to file away; it is a room you can walk back into years later and notice a window you missed the first time.

## A better ritual for remembering what you read

Instead of chasing volume, I now keep a small, curated shelf of books that genuinely shifted how I think, build, or travel:

- **One pull-quote** that captures the book's cadence in a single breath.
- **A handful of honest notes** written within a week of closing the back cover, before memory polishes the rough edges away.
- **A visual spine** so browsing the shelf feels closer to scanning wood ledges in a quiet room than scrolling a spreadsheet.

Some months I finish three books. Other months I spend six weeks lingering inside fifty pages of Calvino or Kleppmann. Both count as a good month.
